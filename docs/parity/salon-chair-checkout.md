What the pages say:

- Fresha, card terminal: choose Card terminal → Pay now; the amount shows on the terminal and the client inserts, taps or uses a wallet. "If a payment fails or a terminal payment is cancelled during checkout, the sale will automatically be saved as Draft"; you can reopen it and pick another method. Clients can tip on the terminal "as long as tipping is enabled in your settings". The page does not mention refunds.
- Fresha, multiple methods: "Split payments let you take payment for a single sale using multiple payment methods, such as a card terminal and cash"; you enter an amount per method until the balance is paid.
- Fresha, tips: tips can be added on the point-of-sale tip screen, on a card terminal, on a self-checkout page, or later online. "Tips are split proportionally based on the value of the services each team member provided during the sale", editable at checkout or after. Preset percentages are set in settings ("no more than three values"). Tips are reviewed in the Finance summary report. The page does not say whether tips count toward commission.
- Fresha, low stock: a low-stock limit and a reorder quantity per product; an alert when the limit is reached and a Monday 9am summary to the account holder; purchase orders can be filled in and sent to suppliers.
- Stripe: tips are either on-reader (the reader shows three suggestions before the card is presented; the confirmed PaymentIntent's `amount` includes the tip and `amount_details.tip.amount` carries it) or on-receipt. A tip the business decides itself goes into the PaymentIntent amount. On-reader tipping is a Terminal Configuration set on the account or a location; `process_config[skip_tipping]` hides the screen for one payment. Stripe also lets a `tip-eligible amount` limit percentage tips to the services on a sale — its own example is a salon selling haircuts and shampoo. The simulated reader comes with the server-driven integration; `present_payment_method` simulates the card being tapped; 4242424242424242 approves and 4000000000000002 is declined with `card_declined`.

## Money: whose account

Every call to Stripe carries the salon's own connected account (the one Settings › Integrations connects). The reader, its Location, its PaymentIntents and its refunds are all on that account; Twomiah holds nothing. The phone app takes no cards and has no new screen: the card is tapped on the reader.

## Decisions and limits to know

- **Off by default.** Two new not-ready (hidden) feature ids: `chair_checkout` (readers, card payments, refunds to card) and `salon_retail` (products, stock, retail lines). Mounted at `/api/chair-checkout` and `/api/salon-retail`. The new tips report rides on the existing hidden `salon_reports`.
- **Who can do what (server-enforced):** taking a card, cancelling one, listing readers: `invoices:update` **or the Front Desk's own narrow right `cardreader:use`** (owner's decision, follow-up 10/30). Pairing or removing a reader and tapping a test card: `company:update` (owner/admin). Refunding to the card: `payments:delete`. Selling retail onto a sale: `invoices:update` **or the Front Desk's `retail:sell`** (a new walk-in sale also `invoices:create` or `retail:sell`); retail list for the till: `invoices:read`; the full catalogue with cost, products, stock received/corrected: `inventory:read/create/update` (a manager holds these). A stylist holds none of them. **The Front Desk can take a card and sell retail with those two narrow rights and nothing more** (no pairing a reader, no refunds, no catalogue or stock, no general invoice editing); proof `apps/unified/tests/salon-front-desk-till.test.ts`.
- **A refund gives the tip back (owner's decision, follow-up 10/30).** Refunding the rest of a reader payment — the refund that makes it fully refunded — returns the tip with it, always: the same Stripe refund carries sale + tip back to the card and the refund line records a **negative tip**, which takes it off the stylist's tips (the Tips report is net of tips given back, dated when they were given back). A **part**-refund leaves the tip unless the person chooses to give it back (the whole tip still outstanding goes, once; choosing again returns nothing more). The sale's ordinary Record Refund (cash, or a card taken some other way) follows the same rule through a "Give back the tip too" tick. A card refunded from Stripe's own dashboard, or by the shared Online Payments refund (`POST /api/stripe/refund`), gives the tip back the same way when it returns the rest of the sale (row 10, 10/31: the shared refund now sends sale + tip and names the tip; a part refund leaves it). Proof: three tests in `apps/unified/tests/salon-chair-checkout.test.ts` and `tests/salon-tip-split.test.ts`.
- **A card taken on a sale that was voided meanwhile** is marked "needs review" with a sentence telling a person to refund it in Stripe; it is never recorded on a void sale. A card taken after the sale was paid some other way is recorded (the sale shows more paid than owed) rather than lost.
- **Editing a sale's lines in the general invoice form keeps the retail links (row 12)** — see the table. The shared package gained one optional hook (`InvoiceOptions.lineEdit`) the salon turns on; other verticals are unchanged. A refund puts units back only when the person chooses (row 12).
- **Retail on a walk-in sale can name a stylist (row 12)**; one that names nobody earns nobody.
- **A mistyped pairing code** still costs a spare (empty) Location on the salon's Stripe account. Harmless.
- **US only, dollars only** (Stripe's WisePOS E / S700 server-driven readers; the salon's address is sent to Stripe once to create the Location).
- **Not verified against real Stripe:** everything above was proved against Stripe's API as documented, answered by a fake in `tests/foodKit.ts` (nothing leaves the machine). The test helper's `amount_tip` parameter (used by the tests to simulate an on-reader tip) is the bar's usage and is **not on the Stripe testing page I read**. **A physical reader has not been tried.** Owner's check before switching `chair_checkout` on for a real salon, with the reader and the salon's Stripe account in test mode: (1) pair the reader in Settings › Card readers with the code the reader shows; (2) take a $1.00 card payment with a $0.25 desk tip and confirm one payment with a 0.25 tip appears on the sale and in Stripe; (3) take another and choose the tip on the reader's screen; (4) press cancel on the reader mid-payment; (5) decline (a test card ending 0002); (6) refund to card and see it in Stripe; (7) unplug the reader and confirm the desk is told it did not answer.
- Proving tests: `apps/unified/tests/salon-chair-checkout.test.ts` (15, three of them the tip refunds) — seat rights; pairing on the salon's own account, one Location; approve with a tip, recorded once, tip on the report; on-reader tip; six polls at once; decline / cancel / timeout; cancel racing an approval; offline reader and bad amounts refused before money moves; split cash + gift card + card; paid-meanwhile and voided-meanwhile; refund to card through Stripe; wrong Stripe account, no Stripe key, a busy reader. `apps/unified/tests/salon-retail.test.ts` (9) — seat rights; product rules; retail line, tax and stock; the last bottle sold once; removal and void return stock; walk-in sale; stock received/corrected with who and why; retail commission and the retail report; the general form's rewrite. Both fail without the change (no routes, no tables).
- Screens (Retail page, Card reader panel, Sell retail panel, Settings › Card readers, Tips in Client reports) are type-checked and built but **not click-tested in a browser**.
- Shared packages: only the feature registry gained two hidden entries. Nothing else outside `apps/unified`.

## Row 12 (10/31) — what is and is not built

Built: low-stock email and Monday summary, reorder quantity, suppliers, purchase orders (draft from low stock, email, receive line by line), web barcode scan, counts, transfers between the salon's own places, walk-in sale naming a stylist, invoice-edit keeps retail links, refund → put units back. Proof: `apps/unified/tests/salon-retail-stock.test.ts` (10) — fails without the change (run both ways: server files reverted, 0 of 10 pass). Screens (Retail › "Orders, suppliers and counts", the scan boxes, the put-back panel) are type-checked and built but **not seen in a real browser**, and no physical scanner or real mailbox was used.

Limits to know: the low-stock email is sent within moments of a stock change made in the app and otherwise by a pass every 15 minutes; a delivery cannot exceed what is still expected on a line; a received order cannot be cancelled; the commission sweep that catches missed payments only looks at bills with a visit, so a walk-in sale's commission depends on the payment door calling the earner (it does for every payment route tested).

**Not built:** barcode scanning with the phone's camera. The phone app has the camera permission and the `expo-camera` package but no scanner screen, and its More menu is generated from the desktop menu; a native scan-and-count screen (camera, `/api/salon-retail-stock/scan`, offline-safe count) has not been written or tested on a phone.

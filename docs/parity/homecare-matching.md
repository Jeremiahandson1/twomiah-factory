# Home care parity: caregiver-to-client matching and suggested caregivers

Research date: 2026-10-30. All wording is paraphrased from the pages listed.

## Capabilities

| Recommended caregivers shown for an open shift as a list of qualified candidates | AxisCare | [CEO interview](https://axiscare.com/?p=34324) | **matches.** `GET /api/scheduling/suggest-caregivers` ranks every active caregiver by score and tier (excellent, good, maybe, avoid) for a client, date and time (`server/crm/homecare/scheduling/schedulingHub.ts`), and the Schedule Hub shows it when a visit is being created (`web/src/crm/pages/homecare/schedule/SchedulingHub.tsx`). An open shift's smart-fill hands the shift's client and times to this same ranking. The open-shift page's own list of free caregivers (`eligible-caregivers`) is not ranked. | AxisCare factors: skills, overtime status, past scheduling history, client preferences (e.g. pet allergy), caregiver preferences, availability, travel distance | AxisCare | [scheduling page](https://axiscare.com/scheduling) | **matches**, except caregivers' own preferences. Factors we use: skills (new, `care_match_prefs`), the client's preferred and do-not-use caregivers, weekly hours against the caregiver's maximum and a warning near overtime, past visits with this client, availability and approved time off, distance (straight line from saved coordinates), clashing visits and expired credentials; plus gender and language (new, and not on this leader's list). Not scored: a caregiver's own stated preferences (the profile stores preferred hours as text but nothing reads it). Skills, gender and language are proven in `tests/homecare-depth-matching.test.ts`; of the older factors only the clash flag is pinned (`tests/homecare-scheduling.test.ts`) and expired credentials (`tests/homecare-depth-people.test.ts`). |

Only the client's do-not-use list and approved time off or blackout days remove a caregiver from the list, unless `includeBlocked=true` is sent. A hard filter would need the owner to say which needs are truly must-have; skills are the likeliest. |

Older code; the 35-hour warning in the ranking has no test of its own. The schedule screen shows only the first four reasons for each suggestion. |

| Visit Optimizer (premium) bulk-assigns vacant visits using availability, client preferences, skills, service area, proximity, travel time, mileage and continuity; priorities are configurable | AlayaCare | [Visit Optimizer news](https://www.homecaremag.com/news/alayacare-launches-visit-optimizer-automation-vacant-visit-scheduling) | **not doing.** There is an auto-fill (`POST /api/scheduling/auto-fill`, with a dry run that `tests/homecare-scheduling.test.ts` runs) that gives each open shift to the best-scoring free caregiver by hours, past visits and distance. ## Gaps and uncertainty

- Reasons shown per suggestion: not found for any leader. AlayaCare's Employee Finder post and the AxisCare sources describe the selection but say nothing about showing a score or reason per candidate.
- Gender preference and language as explicit factors: not found as named factors in any AxisCare or WellSky source. AlayaCare's language claim is marketing copy only. Gender could sit under "client preferences" but is not stated.
- Certifications as a matching factor: AxisCare's sources list skills but not certifications. AlayaCare says "skills and abilities" only.
- WellSky: the specific match criteria are not named on the page read.
- The AxisCare product tours were not opened, only their titles.
- AxisCare's [overview page](https://axiscare.com/overview/) mentions automated visit scheduling with smarter pairing but no factors.

## Status summary

- Built, hidden and marked not ready (`care_match_prefs`): a client can record the skills they need, a preferred caregiver gender and a preferred language; a caregiver can have a gender and languages recorded (entered on the Optimizer screen, `web/src/crm/pages/homecare/shifts/CompanyOptimizer.tsx`). Suggested caregivers then score those factors and give a plain reason for each (a tick or a warning), and a miss lowers the score without hiding anyone. 5 tests (`tests/homecare-depth-matching.test.ts`); I read them but did not run them. On paper this beats the leaders on showing a reason per suggestion, since none documents one, but the reasons are thin and the schedule screen shows only the first four.
- Left unbuilt: hard filters, caregivers' own preferences, departments and seniority, AI scheduling, filtering open shifts by what a caregiver qualifies for, and any use of the new traits (skills, gender, language) in auto-fill or the open-shift broadcast (left alone on purpose). Follow-up 10/30: auto-fill now never offers or assigns a caregiver on the client's do-not-use list, on approved time off, or outside the days and hours they gave, by the same rule the suggestion list reads (`server/crm/homecare/core/caregiverFit.ts`; `tests/homecare-followup-1030.test.ts`).
- Judgement calls for the owner: the score cuts (120 for a missing skill, 100 for the wrong gender, 40 for the wrong language) are my choices; skills must be typed the same way on the client and the caregiver (capital letters do not matter, but "Dementia care" and "dementia" do not match); gender is female or male only; a client who prefers English needs nothing recorded.
- The owner must verify by hand: enter real skills, genders and languages for a few caregivers and clients and check the ranking feels right. Nothing was tried on real data.

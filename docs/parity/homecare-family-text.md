# Home care parity: family and client notifications on caregiver clock in/out

Researched 2026-10-30. Headline: none of the three leaders' pages that were opened document an automatic family text or email when a caregiver clocks in or out. What they do document is a family portal plus account-level emails.

## Gaps and uncertainty
- Automatic text, email or app message to a family member or client when a caregiver clocks in or out: not found for AxisCare, AlayaCare or WellSky. AlayaCare's system notification list covers only employee-facing items and explicitly lacks client notifications; AlayaCare's late clock-in notification is described as legacy and not triggerable.
- Quiet hours: not found for any leader.
- Sender phone number for family texts (shared vs dedicated): not found for any leader.
- Family opt-in or consent capture wording: not found; WellSky and AlayaCare describe office-granted access only.
- help.axiscare.com was not reachable, so AxisCare may document more than its marketing pages show.
- The AlayaCare Family Portal overview article could not be fetched, and the notification channel list for the portal permission is unconfirmed (the Core and Advanced article says channels are not named).
- Third-party tools (for example SMS add-ons for WellSky) appeared in search results but were not opened and are not counted.

## Status summary
- Built: a text to a client's relatives when the caregiver clocks in and when they clock out, only for relatives the office marked as having said yes (and who have a phone number), from the agency's own texting service. One text per visit, relative and event, so a replayed or twice-sent punch cannot text twice. Tested in `tests/homecare-depth-family.test.ts` with a fake texting service, including through the real clock-in and clock-out routes.
- Quiet hours are an agency setting (follow-up 10/30), 9 PM–8 AM until the agency changes them on the Family Portal screen; no text is sent in them (recorded as skipped, never sent later; `tests/homecare-depth-family.test.ts`). A rule fixed in code: a punch that reaches us more than 30 minutes late (for example one saved on a phone with no signal) is skipped. A relative who is skipped is not told afterwards.
- Not built: email or app messages to families, storing the wording the relative agreed to, a screen showing which texts were sent or skipped (the records exist in the database only), and editable text wording.
- The owner must check by hand: switch the feature on for a test agency, tick the yes for a relative with a real mobile number, clock a test caregiver in and out during the day and confirm both texts arrive from the agency's number; then reply STOP and confirm the next clock-in sends nothing to that phone. Also confirm the agency is happy to rely on a ticked box as the relative's consent.

## Parity build, 10/31 — row 7: family requests and notifications
Status: **built in part**, behind `care_family_comms` (hidden; not ready until the owner has tried it in a real browser and with a real texting number). It works with `care_family_texts`.
- **Requests.** A relative with the messaging switch asks for a visit change, a service change or a new visit (a day, and what they need). It becomes an office task (the CRM task list, due the next day), an in-app notice to the office, and a count on the "Open requests" tile on the new **Family messages** page. The office marks it done or declined with a note; the relative sees the state and the note, and the task is closed with it. The same words twice from the same relative are one request.
- **Notices by email too.** A relative who asked for the arrival and finish notice by email (a separate yes) gets it; email is not held for quiet hours (it does not wake anyone) but a late punch is still not news.
- **The yes keeps its exact words.** The words the relative agreed to (with the agency's name) are stored with the yes and shown to the office; a yes recorded before this change says so.
- **Family messages screen.** Every arrival and finish notice, text or email, with who, which visit, and the result. A held text says why (quiet hours; reached us more than 30 minutes late); a failed one says the texting service could not send it. Filter by dates (up to 92) and result.
- **The agency's own wording** for the arrival and finish text, with {caregiver} {client} {time} {agency}; an unknown tag is refused on save so a typo never reaches a family; the STOP line is kept.
- **Portal emails** in the agency's words (account created, forgot password, access granted, access revoked); the last two can be switched off. Forgot password is new (a link from the sign-in page, good once, for an hour; the same answer for an unknown address).
- **STOP honoured by our code.** See the table above.
- Tests: `tests/homecare-parity-family-comms.test.ts` (11). The server test cannot run without the change (the module, routes and columns do not exist).
- **Row 7 extras (10/31, `care_family_comms`):** (1) the arrival / finish notice as an app notice on the relative's own phone or browser (web push, turned on from the portal's Notices tab); (2) the agency's own alert rules (Alert rules tab; see the table); (3) the email yes keeps its exact words like the text yes. Tests: `tests/homecare-parity-family-alerts.test.ts` (10; cannot run without the change). Not seen in a real browser or with a real push service.
- **NOT built:** (1) an installable family phone app (push goes to the portal in the browser); (2) a rule for a caregiver who has not arrived at all; (3) the request does not offer "pick one of their upcoming visits" from a list, only a free day and words; (4) the "Open requests" tile is on the Family messages page, not the office dashboard; (5) a relative cannot yet switch their own notices on or off from the portal (the office records it, as for texts); (6) not seen in a real browser, a real inbox or with a real texting number.
